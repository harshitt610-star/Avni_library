The Cosmic Adventure

Dual Narrative System

On a breezy autumn rooftop in Jaipur, eight-year-old Tara unboxes her grandfather’s weathered brass telescope. Expecting only the familiar silver dust of the Moon, she instead tunes into shimmering celestial ripples that hum softly in two interlaced tongues: lyrical Hindi verses and crisp English astronomy facts.

When the Great Bear constellation—known across centuries as the Saptarshi (सप्तर्षि)—starts glowing an urgent sapphire shade, Tara realizes that modern light pollution in the city below is silencing the stars’ generational folktales. Guided by an animated astral owl named Nakshatra, Tara embarks on a whimsical nocturnal journey between scientific constellations and classical mythological folklore.

"When science gives you eyes to see the universe, stories give you the heart to understand it."

Parent & Classroom Discussion Kit

1. Pre-Reading Spark: "What does the sky look like from your home?"

Prompt children to observe the night sky before opening Chapter 1. Discuss why city skies have fewer visible stars than countryside hills, opening an easy dialogue on light pollution and environmental science.

2. Bilingual Discovery: Matching Hindi and English Star Names

Have learners connect: Taara (तारा) = Star, Dhruva (ध्रुव) = North Star / Constant, Aakashganga (आकाशगंगा) = Milky Way. Encourage speaking both pronunciations aloud.

3. Hands-On Activity: DIY Cardboard Tube Telescope

Follow Tara's method on Page 14 using recycled kitchen paper towel tubes and pinhole constellation cards to project Ursa Major on a darkened bedroom wall.
